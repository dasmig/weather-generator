Third-party data in weather-generator. How each part is built is described in
doc/data.md.

1. CRU TS 4.10 (resources/lite/climate.tsv, resources/full/climate.tsv and the
   compiled-in copy, dasmig/weathergen_data.hpp: the monthly normals of
   temperature, diurnal range, precipitation, wet days, cloud and, from
   vapour pressure, humidity)
   Climatic Research Unit, University of East Anglia: the monthly 0.5-degree
   grids of 1991-2020, https://crudata.uea.ac.uk/cru/data/hrg/cru_ts_4.10/
   Harris, I., Osborn, T.J., Jones, P. and Lister, D. (2020). Version 4 of
   the CRU TS monthly high-resolution gridded multivariate climate dataset.
   Scientific Data 7:109. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-020-0453-3
   Licensed under the Open Database License (ODbL) v1.0, with the individual
   contents under the Database Contents License (DbCL) v1.0.
   https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1.0/
   https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/dbcl/1.0/
   The climate tables are a database adapted from it by
   scripts/prepare_climate.py: 30-year means of each calendar month, and
   2-degree block means for the lite tier.

2. CRU CL 2.0 (the same files: elevation, wind, and each cell's land
   centroid and area)
   Climatic Research Unit, University of East Anglia: the 10-minute mean
   climate grids of 1961-1990, https://crudata.uea.ac.uk/cru/data/hrg/tmc/
   New, M., Lister, D., Hulme, M. and Makin, I. (2002). A high-resolution
   data set of surface climate over global land areas. Climate Research
   21:1-25.
   Licensed under the ODbL v1.0 and the DbCL v1.0, as above.

3. Natural Earth (resources/lite/land.tsv, resources/full/land.tsv and the
   compiled-in copy: which half-degree cells hold land)
   Natural Earth 1:10m Physical Vectors, land and minor islands,
   https://www.naturalearthdata.com/. Public domain.

4. GHCN-Daily (the model's constants in dasmig/weathergen.hpp)
   Global Historical Climatology Network - Daily, NOAA National Centers for
   Environmental Information, https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/global-historical-climatology-network-daily
   Menne, M.J., Durre, I., Vose, R.S., Gleason, B.E. and Houston, T.G.
   (2012). An overview of the Global Historical Climatology Network-Daily
   database. Journal of Atmospheric and Oceanic Technology 29:897-910.
   No station records are included: scripts/calibrate.py fits a few dozen
   constants on them (rain persistence, gamma shapes, temperature spreads
   and autocorrelation, wind, snow and thunder probabilities), and the
   station lists in scripts/ name the stations used.

Because the climate tables are adapted from ODbL sources, the ODbL's terms
apply to them. In short, and not as legal advice (the licence texts govern):

- Weather generated from the data, such as the days returned by the
  library, is a "Produced Work" (ODbL section 4.3). Anything you publish that
  is made from it must carry a notice saying it uses data from the Climatic
  Research Unit, University of East Anglia (CRU TS 4.10 and CRU CL 2.0) under
  the ODbL. There is no share-alike requirement for Produced Works.

- If you publicly use or distribute the database itself, or a database
  adapted from it (for example these files with your changes, an extension
  layer built from them, or a program with the compiled-in copy), you must
  credit the sources above, keep this notice, and offer that database under
  the ODbL v1.0 (share-alike, section 4.4). You may not restrict it with
  technical measures unless you also offer it without them (section 4.7).

The library's code, and the curated constants marked as such in
dasmig/weathergen.hpp (cloud and humidity on wet days, the sleet threshold),
are under the MIT licence of the library.
